Fintech pitches emphasize growth, margins, and regulatory posture. Investors should still ask whether the architecture can survive scale, audit scrutiny, and a failed vendor. The goal is not to demand perfection. It is to price rebuild risk before it becomes a post-close surprise.

1. Ledger design and reconciliation discipline

Ask how money movement is recorded, not just how it is displayed. A credible stack has an append-only ledger or event log, idempotent posting, and daily reconciliation to bank and partner statements. If balances are mutable rows without an audit trail, diligence should treat that as structural risk.

2. Custody and funds-flow boundaries

Understand where customer funds sit, which entity holds licenses, and what happens when a banking or custody partner changes terms. Diagrams that skip settlement timing or commingling rules are a signal that operations may be carrying the real architecture.

3. Identity, fraud, and authorization depth

Authentication is table stakes. Investors should examine step-up controls, device binding, admin authorization, and how privileged actions are logged. Fintech incidents often start with weak internal tooling rather than consumer-facing login.

4. Data lineage for regulatory and investor reporting

Request one example of how a regulatory or board number is produced end to end. If the path crosses ad hoc spreadsheets, unmanaged replicas, or unnamed “analytics databases,” reporting risk is higher than the deck suggests.

5. Vendor and blockchain dependency assumptions

Many products combine custodians, card processors, chain indexers, and internal services. Diligence should identify single points of failure, contract exit cost, and what happens when an external read disagrees with the application database.

6. Security investments that lag product velocity

Post-PMF security work is expensive because it interrupts live flows. Ask whether secrets management, environment separation, dependency patching, and incident response are funded in the current plan—not deferred to “after the next release.”

How to use this in a diligence session

Pick three questions tied to money, access, and reporting. Request artifacts, not narratives: a ledger sample, an on-call runbook, and a reconciliation report. The quality of those artifacts usually predicts whether the team can scale architecture with revenue.
